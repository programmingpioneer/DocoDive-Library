    1. Our approach to accessibility

    Reading, finding resources and managing an account should be possible without unnecessary barriers. We aim to use clear language, meaningful page structure, readable layouts and controls that can be identified and operated with different input methods.

    Accessibility work includes considering keyboard access, visible focus, text contrast, page reflow, labels and information that does not depend on colour alone. These are practical goals for ongoing improvements, not a statement that every page or feature currently meets them.

    We have not published a comprehensive independent accessibility audit or a certification for the entire service. This statement does not claim full conformance with a particular accessibility standard. User-contributed documents and external tools can introduce additional barriers.

    2. Using the website

    Your browser and operating system may offer settings that make the site more comfortable to use, such as text enlargement, zoom, contrast preferences and reduced motion. The legal and help pages use a reading layout that reflows on smaller screens and includes links to each section.

    • Use your browser's zoom or text settings to enlarge content. If enlarged text hides a control or creates an unusable layout, please report the page.
    • Use the Tab and Shift+Tab keys to move through interactive elements. Enter generally follows a focused link; buttons can usually be activated with Enter or Space.
    • Use headings and landmarks available through your screen reader or reading tools to navigate longer pages.
    • Some wide information tables can be scrolled horizontally on small screens. The surrounding page should continue to fit the screen.

    These suggestions describe common browser behaviour and do not guarantee compatibility with every device or assistive technology. If a task is blocked, tell us which step and control caused the difficulty so we can reproduce it.

    3. Library documents and reading features

    DocoDive includes documents contributed by different sources. The accessibility of each file can vary. Some PDFs are scanned images without selectable text; others may have missing tags, unclear reading order, small print, images without descriptions or tables that are difficult to interpret with assistive technology.

    An online reader or downloadable file does not automatically make a document accessible. We cannot guarantee that every library item has an accessible equivalent or that we have the rights or source material needed to convert it. Browser PDF viewers can also behave differently.

    If a particular resource creates a barrier, send its URL and describe the format or assistance that would help. We can review the listing and consider whether another source, edition or format is available. Availability and permissions affect what we can provide.

    If you contribute a document, prefer a clear source file with selectable text, meaningful headings, sensible reading order and descriptions for information conveyed only through images. Accurate descriptions and edition details also help readers choose a suitable resource.

    4. Third-party tools and content

    Social sign-in, browser document viewers, externally linked websites and optional support chat are provided by other services. Their interfaces and accessibility may change independently of DocoDive. We cannot guarantee the behaviour of every external tool.

    If optional chat is difficult to use, you can contact us by email without enabling it. If social sign-in prevents access, explain the provider and step involved in your support message. Do not include passwords or verification codes.

    5. Report a barrier or request assistance

    Email support@programmingpioneer.com with the subject “Accessibility feedback.” Helpful details include:

    • The page or document URL and the task you were trying to complete.
    • What prevented you from continuing and what you expected to happen.
    • Your browser, device and assistive technology, if relevant and if you are comfortable sharing them.
    • An alternative format or way of receiving information that would help.

    You do not need to tell us about a diagnosis or provide medical information. We use the details you share to understand and investigate the barrier. If a screenshot is useful, remove personal information that is not needed for the report.

    We will consider the report and what assistance or change is reasonably possible. The time needed can depend on the feature, the file's source and whether another provider is involved. We do not promise a fixed resolution time or that every resource can be supplied in every format.

    6. Ongoing improvements

    Reports help us identify problems that affect reading, navigation and essential account tasks. We aim to prioritise barriers that prevent people from completing those tasks and to consider accessibility when revising interfaces or introducing features.

    This statement may change as we learn more about the service's accessibility and make improvements. The update date appears above. For other support routes, see contact and support; for how feedback information is handled, read the privacy policy.
